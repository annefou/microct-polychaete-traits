MICRO-CT SPECIES-TRAIT EBV FEASIBILITY TEST (prototype, 2026-10-22)
=====================================================================
Question: can open micro-CT volumes of marine invertebrates be segmented reproducibly,
turned into morphological traits, and matched to independently published measurements?

VERDICT: FEASIBLE WITH CONDITIONS
  - Size traits (max dimension / Feret diameter) are reproducible: they vary by <=0.5 %
    over a threshold sweep of x0.8-x1.2 and agree across Otsu, Li and Triangle thresholds
    for 2 of 3 scans. They agree with published values to within +8 to +12 %, with a
    consistent positive bias.
  - Volume, surface area, sphericity and width are NOT yet robust with a global threshold.
    The sweep changes them by 15-70 %. They need calibrated grey values, standard staining
    and protocols, or a trained segmentation model.
  - The published reference values are coarse (Table 1 gives 0.1 mm precision, "ca" widths)
    and come from one paper. This is a plausibility check, not a validation.

DATA USED (all CC0)
  Faulwetter S, Vasileiadou A, Kouratoras M, Dailianis T, Arvanitidis C (2013) Micro-computed
  tomography: introducing new dimensions to taxonomy. ZooKeys 263:1-45.
  doi:10.3897/zookeys.263.4261. Data: Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.84m54 = Zenodo record 4959912
  (CC0; 9 polychaete scans, 46 MB - 1.7 GB each, BMP/PNG slice stacks + SkyScan .log).
  Specimens processed (downloads 47 + 64 + 52 MB zipped; deleted after the run):
    Eunice_sp_I2E   juvenile Eunice sp., whole animal, iodine-stained, 2.162 um px, 473 slices
    Eunice_sp_PTA   juvenile Eunice sp., whole animal, PTA-stained,    2.298 um px, 542 slices
    Eunice_roussaei dissected mid-body parapodium, HMDS-dried, in air,  2.878 um px, 451 slices
  Published reference values:
    - ZooKeys Table 1, "Size (mm)" (footnote: "maximum dimension (in any direction) of the
      scanned part of the specimen"): I2E 1.8, PTA 2.2, E. roussaei 2.9.
    - Results text: Eunice sp. juveniles have "width ca 0.3 mm".

PIPELINE (pipeline/traits_pipeline.py; classical image processing, no AI; CPU 1.5-7.5 min per specimen)
  load raw values -> voxel size from .log -> isotropic resampling -> 3D median + Gaussian
  -> Otsu -> 3D closing -> largest component -> hole filling per slice along each axis + 3D
  -> traits: volume, surface area (marching cubes), 3D max Feret, 2D max Feret in the PC1-2
  plane, PCA extents, elongation, sphericity, body centre-line length (skeleton of the body
  after a 13-17 um opening, longest path, smoothed), body width (2 x inscribed radius along
  the centre-line, mid half), and one structure-specific trait: volume of the high-density
  class (3-class multi-Otsu inside the mask: jaws, aciculae, chaetae, sclerotised parts).
  Sensitivity: Otsu x{0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.2}, plus Li and Triangle as alternative methods.

  Reading the errors:
  - The bias is +8 to +12 % for all three scans and the 2D Feret gives nearly the same values.
    So it is probably a definition or rounding difference, not noise. Possible causes: authors
    measured a rendered view, excluded antennae or protruding chaetae, or rounded down.
    The paper does not state its measurement method, so this cannot be settled.
  - Width: the published value is approximate ("ca"). Our median body width excludes parapodia.
  - Volume: thresholds chosen by different automatic methods disagree badly for the PTA scan.
    Li and Triangle thresholds also capture the ethanol-filled pipette tip (1.2 mm3 against
    0.14 mm3). Without a fixed protocol, automatic method choice can silently corrupt a trait.

METADATA PITFALLS FOUND (each would silently corrupt an operational trait product)
  1. Anisotropic voxels hidden in the log. "Section to Section Step=2" means the z spacing is
     2 x the stated pixel size (4.32 um, not 2.16 um). If z is treated as isotropic, lengths
     along z come out at half their true value.
  2. Inverted palette. The PTA BMPs store an inverted greyscale palette: converting to grey
     inverts the densities, so raw indices must be used.
  3. Inconsistent metadata. Phyllodoce lineata is listed at 1.645 um px in ZooKeys Table 1
     but 1.096 um px in the dataset README (1.096 is the Phyllodoce sp. value). This was not
     processed here, but it shows the need for machine-readable, authoritative voxel size.
  4. Measurement definitions are free text ("maximum dimension in any direction",
     "width ca"), with no method or uncertainty.

    a reference for AI segmentation. It was not processed.

WHAT AN OPERATIONAL TRAIT EBV SERVICE WOULD NEED
  - Standard acquisition classes, e.g. 1-3 um, 5-10 um and 10-25 um voxels by body size,
    with a declared minimum of voxels per body width (here about 50-110 voxels per 0.24 mm
    width was ample for size traits).
  - Machine-readable metadata travelling with every volume: true 3D voxel spacing (z step),
    bit depth, LUT, reconstruction parameters, staining and preparation (fixative, stain,
    HMDS or wet, medium), scanner, voxel unit. Use NeXus/OME-Zarr or NRRD with headers
    rather than 8-bit BMP stacks plus a log.
  - Calibration: a density phantom or a reference medium in each scan so that thresholds
    transfer between scans. Volume and surface traits are not comparable without it.
  - Trait definitions as controlled vocabulary with method and uncertainty: body length
    (centre-line), max Feret, width at a given segment, volume, hard-part volume. Map them to
    an EBV "species traits" ontology (e.g. OBA / PATO / Darwin Core MeasurementOrFact).
  - Annotations for AI: voxel-level labels (body, parapodia, chaetae, jaws, medium,
    container) on a stratified set of taxa, stains and voxel sizes. Hundreds of labelled
    volumes are needed before a 3D U-Net / nnU-Net is worth training; jaws, chaetae and
    segment counting are where AI adds value over thresholds.
  - FAIR packaging: one RO-Crate per specimen as a FAIR Digital Object, linking the raw
    volume, the processing workflow (versioned, containerised), masks, the trait table
    (DwC-MoF) and the provenance of each trait value, with the specimen voucher, AphiaID
    and the occurrence (collection site).
  - Spatial indexing of specimen collection locations on HEALPix (e.g. order 8-10), so trait
    values aggregate to EBV grid cells. ZooKeys Table 1 already gives coordinates for most
    specimens (e.g. 35.4158 N, 24.9875 E).
  - Quality control: report every trait with its threshold-sweep range (as done here), and
    flag traits whose range exceeds a tolerance (e.g. 5 %).

WHAT HCMR / LIFEWATCHGREECE MICROCT VLAB WOULD CONTRIBUTE
  - A curated, licensed collection of marine invertebrate volumes with consistent protocols:
    the HCMR team (Faulwetter, Keklikoglou, Dailianis, Arvanitidis) produced these data and
    the 2019 best-practice handbook.
  - Matching trait tables already published as DwC datasets (MARBEFES, Glycera), which can
    serve as ground truth for automated pipelines once the volumes are linked.
  - A natural host for the operational e-Service, as a VLab workflow step: volume -> mask ->
    traits -> RO-Crate.
  - Taxonomic expertise to create the AI training annotations. The Spanish group's
    AI-based 3D pattern recognition on polychaetes could supply or benchmark the models.
  These contributions could not be checked against the live VLab, which was unreachable.
